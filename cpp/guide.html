User guide

Eleven pages, in the order they are worth reading. The first four are the path from nothing to a strategy running against a broker; the next four are the ones that stop a live account losing money for a reason nobody saw coming; the last three are the machinery underneath, for extending the library rather than using it.

Nothing here repeats the API reference. Each page exists because the reasoning behind a decision does not fit in a \brief, and because the reasoning is the part that is expensive to rediscover.

Start here

  • Architecture — the layers, the dependency rule, and why a strategy holds no session.
  • Writing a strategy — the struct you write, its handlers, and why it emits a target rather than an order.
  • Binding a broker — putting a session behind it, setting the limits, and running with no broker at all first.
  • Market data — bars and paging, the two timestamp conventions that prevent a silent lookahead, the price grid, and the calendar's three answers.

Before you trust a number, or an account

  • The analytics layer: two numeric worlds and four conventions — two numeric worlds, four conventions, and the smoothing constants that packages disagree about.
  • Reading a backtest honestly — what the fill model assumes, what it does not model at all, and which figures to distrust first.
  • Running a strategy live — the three stops, the two gates that deliberately refuse to trade, and the supervisor grace period that turns the cleanest shutdown into the worst one.
  • Recovering after a crash — the journal, the five verdicts, and how to read them at 3am without talking yourself into the wrong one.

Underneath

  • The broker port — BrokerTraits, BrokerCaps, order identity, and the capability table. Read this to add a broker.
  • Talking to a broker directly — the concrete KIS, Kiwoom and Toss clients, the order-safety rules, and the examples.
  • Transport and request pacing — the HTTPS transport, the pacer, the five traffic classes, and how differently the two brokers report a refusal.
